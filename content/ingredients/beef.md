culturalContext: Nikujaga, beef and potato simmered in a sweet soy broth, is often called the taste of a mother's cooking in Japan; western Japan usually makes it with beef, the east with pork. In Sichuan cooking, beef goes into mapo tofu as mince and into water-boiled beef as thin slices. In Naples, a large piece of beef cooks for hours in genovese, with half again its weight in onions, and in ragù; in Oaxaca, thin salted tasajo is grilled for tlayudas, and chichilo is usually made with beef. Levantine kafta and kibbeh often use beef or lamb.

Beef is rich and savoury, and how it's cut decides how it's cooked. Thin slices cook in a minute or two and stay tender; mince browns and seasons a whole sauce; tougher cuts need long, gentle braising.
