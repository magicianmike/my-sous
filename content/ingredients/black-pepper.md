culturalContext: In Punjabi cooking, black pepper is a spice in its own right and one of the essentials of garam masala; mutton karahi is often finished with freshly ground peppercorns. In Levantine cooking it's the steady background seasoning of meat dishes such as kibbeh and kafta, usually with allspice and cinnamon. Neapolitan taralli and Easter casatiello are made with lard and plenty of pepper, though chile is the main heat in much Southern Italian cooking. In Oaxaca, black pepper is ground with dried chiles into the marinade for cecina.

Black pepper brings a sharp, woody heat that's gentler than chile and builds as you add it. Freshly cracked, it's pungent and aromatic; cooked for a long time, it mellows into the background.
