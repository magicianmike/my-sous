culturalContext: In Japan, cod is a winter fish, simmered in hot pots such as Akita's tarajiru, cod and leek in a miso broth, and served with ponzu and grated daikon. Many Japanese recipes, such as miso-marinated saikyo-yaki, use black cod (sablefish), which isn't a true cod. In Punjabi fish curry, firm white fish such as cod is fried first, then finished in the masala. In Southern Italy, cod mostly arrives as baccalà, salt cod soaked for two days, then fried for the Neapolitan Christmas Eve table or baked with potatoes.

Cod is a lean, mild white fish with large, tender flakes. It takes on the flavour of a broth or a marinade, and because it falls apart if it's stirred, it's best cooked gently in thick pieces.
