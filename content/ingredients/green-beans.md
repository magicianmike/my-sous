culturalContext: Sichuan dry-fried green beans are cooked in a hot pan until they wrinkle and scorch in spots, then tossed with pork, chile and Sichuan pepper. In Japanese home cooking they're blanched briefly for goma-ae, a sesame dressing, or added at the end of simmered dishes such as nikujaga. In Levantine cooking, they're braised slowly in tomato and olive oil, with or without meat, for fasolia and loubieh. In Sicily they're tossed in fresh tomato sauce, in Puglia boiled with potatoes for a summer salad, and in Oaxaca they cook in mole verde and chichilo.

Green beans are crisp and grassy, and they change with how they're cooked: blanched briefly, they stay bright and snappy; fried hard, they blister and wrinkle, with scorched spots.
